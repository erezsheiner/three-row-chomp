# Independent bridge from the canonical table to the formal game

This review discharges mathematical dependencies shared by the paper audit. It does not treat a source's assertion that a lemma is “banked” as evidence. ## Definition and uniqueness of the table

`ChompLean/Proof.lean:19–35` defines the current-row prefix C, journal R and union B exactly as in the paper: R(q,r) contains f(a,min(a,r)) for a<q, and C(q,r) contains f(q,b) for b<r. Splitting a<r and r<=a gives the paper's seeded-column journal. `IsMex` means positive, absent, and every smaller positive integer present. `BrouwerRecurrence` has precisely the copy test r<q and f(q−1,r)<q; otherwise it requires this positive MEX. `R56TopTwoHoles.gameF_brouwerRecurrence` discharges that recurrence for the actual-game encoding.

No separate implementation equivalence is needed to identify this mathematical recurrence with gameF. Induct on q and then on r: all earlier rows are equal, so both tables make the same copy decision; in a copy branch the values agree, and in a recomputation branch the journals and already constructed row prefixes agree. Two positive MEX values of the same set are equal, since a strict inequality would put the smaller, absent value in the set. At (0,0), B is empty, hence the value is1; the exact formal base is `R66CeilingBase.gameF_zero_zero`. This proves uniqueness on the triangular domain. It does NOT certify a particular fast program merely because its output agrees on finitely many rows.

## Basic facts P01–P12

1. **P01.** `gameRecurrenceModel.value_gt_index` gives f(q,r)>r; its `diagonal_gt` gives d(q)>q. `not_first_implies_ge_of_encoding game_encodes game_complete` gives f(q,r)>=q in every recomputation branch. The copy branch is below q directly from its test and equality. The predicate “live” must specify whether equality at the death row is included.
2. **P02.** `gameRecurrenceModel.fixed_q_injective`, instantiated by `encoding_fixed_q_injective game_encodes`, applies to every pair of columns <=q, with no extra outcome hypothesis.
3. **P03.** `ChompLean/Proof.diagonalMaximal gameRecurrenceModel` bounds every cell by d(q). If r<q, equality contradicts P02, hence the inequality is strict. This does not imply monotonicity of the sequence of diagonals.
4. **P04.** The second field of the exact `BrouwerRecurrence` supplies all three MEX clauses, including nonmembership, when the copy condition fails. `XGameFOrderedBlockerUnionReconstruction.gameF_visible_isMex` also discharges the branch when f(q,r)>=q, including equality. A weaker model's `mex_no_skip` field alone is insufficient for nonmembership and should not be substituted for this theorem.
5. **P05.** `ChompLean/Proof.diagonal_injective gameRecurrenceModel` gives diagonal injectivity. `gameRecurrenceModel.diagonal_general_no_attack` gives d(i) != f(q,r) for i<r<=q. Both statements concern exact triangular indices.
6. **P06/P07.** `opening_cover` and `disjoint_D_S` partition every positive label between D and S. The definition S(p) is existence of r<p with f(p,r)=p; P02 gives unique r. Every diagonal owner of p has index<p by P01. A death p is a recomputed equality at row p, followed by copies on later rows using `constant_from`. Thus a diagonal label cannot be a death/frozen label. Conversely any copied label can be traced back finitely until its first such equality; equivalently, the complete actual-game encoding of f(q,r)=p<q supplies the row-start P-position and hence f(p,r)=p. This also shows different death labels have different victim columns, because an already frozen column stays constant.
7. **P08.** If r<=a<q, the journal witness a gives f(a,r) in R(q,r), since min(a,r)=r. P04 excludes that label at any later recomputation. This includes a column's birth row, which is not covered by the narrower `encoding_same_lower_no_repeat` statement requiring r<a. Persistent copies are allowed and must never be counted as distinct services.
8. **P09.** The penultimate cell is always a recomputation: f(q−1,q−1)>q−1, so the copy test at q fails. Thus it is >=q. `PenultimateFreezeGlobalKill.gameF_gap_one_kill` with c=q−1 proves it is not q for all q>=3, including the small q=3,4 cases; hence it is >=q+1. Using only `gameF_no_penultimate_freeze` would incorrectly leave q=3,4 unhandled.
9. **P10.** `R56TopTwoHoles.gameF_top_two_positive_holes` identifies v(q) as the first positive hole of the set containing all earlier diagonals and the interior prefix. Its nonmembership clause excludes every past diagonal. This is for q>0, exactly the domain of v(q).
10. **P11.** `R56NeverVanish.recurrence_nonpast_below_diagonal_strict_prefix` proves that a positive non-past label below d(q) occurs in the prefix. The first-hole MEX statement above gives the analogous interior-prefix version below v(q): the past-diagonal arm is ruled out by the non-past hypothesis.
11. **P12.** At the diagonal, the MEX source contains q historical diagonal values and q prefix values, at most2q distinct positive values. A positive MEX is at most2q+1. `R43FutureDiagonalSourceCapacityCollapse.lean` proves this source-capacity bound over the model. The strict lower bound is P01. This is a cardinality argument and makes no claim that the two source sets are disjoint.

## Victim propagation, FFD, and service counts

`ChompLean/Proof.rowStartPropagation` says that if f(p,s)=p and s<t<p, then some host b<s has f(t,b)=p. This is the required P41, with all inequalities strict. No host can serve p twice before its death: the later occurrence is a recomputation and its journal contains the earlier occurrence by P08. Therefore p−s−1 distinct hosts fit below s, proving p<=2s+1.

The publication initially omitted `ClaudeFutureFreezeDiagonal.lean`, SHA256 `f92ad3a06d1adfeec5d0603bc98cb2ad8f1041eb158238e6901d15079ac9d1f8`. Its only import is `ChompLean.Proof`; it is now undergoing an additional strict build and kernel audit. I independently read the entire argument. For each intermediate row t in(s,p), propagation chooses a distinct host b_t<s. Its value at row s is >s, since otherwise persistence would contradict its later value p. It is <d(s), by diagonal maximality and row injectivity. Row-s injectivity makes t↦f(s,b_t) injective into(s,d(s)), so p<=d(s). Equality is excluded by D/S disjointness. The adjacent case p=s+1 follows from d(s)>s and the same disjointness. Thus p<d(s), exactly FFD, without assuming future recurrence regularity. The file's `gameF_futureFreezeDiagonal` is the actual-game specialization; the model-level theorem explicitly requires no-repeat, which the actual game discharges.

FFD and P11 also put p in the row-s prefix. At earlier rows t<s any possible host is <=t<s. Thus EVERY predeath service of a blocker p is strictly left of its victim s. Predeath rows t<p serve p exactly when d(t)>p, because p is never diagonal and diagonal maximality supplies the reverse implication. Every diagonal <=p has owner<p. Hence the number of these rows is p−R(p)=A(p), and no-repeat makes this also the number of distinct private hosts.

For a mortal column h with death r, the birth-row service and rows h+1,...,r−1 give r−h distinct hosts<h. These hosts are all alive after h; any column dead by h permanently carries a label<=h<r. Exactly A(h) columns have died by h, all with victim<h. Hence r−h<=h−A(h), or **r+A(h)<=2h**, the P74 dependency. This counting uses equality-at-death consistently.

## Quantitative premises and conventions

The elementary cell reach bound used in paper service counts is **f(q,r)<=q+r+1** for r<=q. In a recomputation branch its journal has at most q values and its prefix at most r, so the positive MEX is at most q+r+1. In a copy branch its value is <q, which is stronger. In particular, at START N an immortal column h<N has exactly N distinct journal labels: h inherited diagonal seeds and N−h private awards. The seed and private sets are disjoint by MEX nonmembership; private awards never repeat because the column never copies. Each label is <=N+h: earlier seed d(i)<=2i+1<=2h−1 and private award f(q,h)<=q+h+1<=N+h.

The prebooking implication often named P42 is also direct, without a new assumption. Let p be a blocker with victim s, and let h<s be a column not dead before p. At row p its value is at least p, and it is not p by row injectivity, hence exceeds p. MEX coverage of the smaller value p puts p in the journal or left prefix at h. The prefix alternative is impossible because its unique row-p host is s>h. As p is not a diagonal seed, it must be a private earlier award at h. Consequently every older column not privately serving p is an earlier frozen victim. There are A(p) booker hosts and at most A(p)−1 earlier victims, giving s<=2A(p)−1. This is an upper bound on a single victim, not monotonicity of victims by blocker rank.

`R201DiagDoubleSlackCeilingChain.gameF_FloorPlus` states, unconditionally,16<=a and d(a)=k imply3a+5<=2k. In the same module `gameFBlockerCountThrough_AFloor` states26<=v implies v<=3A(v−1)+2. Here `gameFBlockerCountThrough` filters the positive list1,...,n by S(gameF), exactly the positive blocker count by the partition above. Put v=n+1 to obtain A(n)>=(n−1)/3 for n>=25, and consequently liminf A(n)/n>=1/3. No zero-inclusive count is used.

The row-sharp inequality **d(h)+A(h)>=2h+1** is independently proved by counting. Row h contains exactly A(h) values<=h: every blocker p<=h is present at its persistent victim, and any value<=h is such a blocker by the encoding/partition argument. The other h+1−A(h) distinct entries lie among h+1,...,d(h), so h+1−A(h)<=d(h)−h. Rearrangement gives the claim, including h=0.

The stronger upper bound d(a)+10<=2a for a>=37 is NOT discharged by any of these arguments. Its asserted status in the consultation brief is not evidence; the audit keeps that separate obligation open unless an exact proof is supplied.

## Cross-check of the death-swap correction

I independently checked `paper-a/DEATH-SWAP-REPAIR.md`. For mortal a with p=sigma_a, private hosts P have size A(p). Older victims dead by a+1 form V, whose size is A(a+1)−1[p=a+1]: only the possible death at a+1 can have victim a instead of a strictly older column. In the a-element universe of columns<a, P∩V=S and the complement of P∪V is M. Inclusion-exclusion gives A(p)+A(a+1)−a=|S|−|M|+1[p=a+1]. The explicit initial rows give the claimed failure at a=1,p=2. This repairs the identity and does not establish the proposed sign inequality.
